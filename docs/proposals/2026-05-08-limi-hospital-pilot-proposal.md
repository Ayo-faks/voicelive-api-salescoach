## Summary

The Limi Hospital sees children every week — through ENT, paediatrics, family medicine, and psychology — whose communication, articulation, or language development would benefit from structured speech practice between clinical visits. Wulo is a clinician‑supervised speech practice platform that fills that gap: the clinician sets the targets, the child practises with a calm AI buddy in short guided sessions, and the clinician reviews structured progress before the next appointment.

We propose a 12‑week, low‑risk pilot with a single Limi clinic and 15–25 paediatric patients, jointly designed with Limi's clinical team. The goal is not to sell software; it is to produce evidence — adherence, family satisfaction, clinician time saved — that lets Limi decide whether to extend Wulo across the group.

## Why this fits Limi

- **Family‑centred mandate.** Limi positions itself as a multispecialty group *for the family*. Speech and language support is one of the highest‑frequency unmet needs in paediatric outpatient care.
- **Existing referral surface.** ENT, paediatrics, psychology, and physiotherapy already see children who would benefit from structured carryover practice between consultations.
- **Capacity reality.** Specialist time is scarce; appointments are short; carryover at home is inconsistent. Wulo extends the clinician's reach without adding clinician hours.
- **Brand fit.** "41 years of patient‑centred care" pairs naturally with a tool whose explicit posture is *clinician‑led, not clinician‑replacing*.

## What Wulo is

Wulo is a web‑based, clinician‑supervised speech practice platform. In one loop:

1. The clinician (or a designated SLT/therapist) selects or authors short exercises for a child.
2. The child practises in 3–5 minute guided sessions with a child‑friendly AI buddy. The system gives gentle prompts, retries, and word‑level pronunciation feedback.
3. Parents see what was practised and how it went, in plain language.
4. The clinician opens a structured review before the next appointment — what the child attempted, where it stalled, suggested next steps — without listening back to recordings.

**Wulo is not** a diagnostic tool, a replacement for a clinician, or an unsupervised consumer app. It is a practice and review layer that sits between visits.

Built on Microsoft Azure (Azure AI Foundry, Azure Speech, Azure Container Apps). Live today at `sen.wulo.ai`.

## Success measures

We propose to measure four things, agreed in writing at kickoff:

1. **Adherence** — % of enrolled children who complete ≥2 practice sessions/week.
2. **Clinician time** — minutes spent reviewing each child before the next appointment, vs. baseline.
3. **Family experience** — short post‑pilot survey (clarity of role, confidence supporting practice, willingness to continue).
4. **Clinical signal** — clinician‑rated change in carryover quality at the next visit (qualitative, not diagnostic).

A pilot is successful, in our view, if at least three of these four show a meaningful positive signal and the clinical lead would recommend continuing.

## After the pilot

Three options, Limi's choice:

- **Stop.** Limi keeps the evidence pack. No fees. No lock‑in.
- **Extend** to one more Limi clinic on the same terms for a further 12 weeks.
- **Adopt** group‑wide under a paid agreement. Indicative pricing for a multi‑site Nigerian hospital group is **₦4.5M–₦7.5M/year** depending on number of clinicians and active children, billed annually. Final pricing would follow the pilot evidence.
