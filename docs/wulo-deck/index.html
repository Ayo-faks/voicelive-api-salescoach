01 · The Problem

A one-hour session was being eaten by missing context.

Therapists were forced to reconstruct the week before they could treat the child in front of them.

  • Home practice was inconsistent, undocumented, and hard to verify.
  • Parents wanted to help, but lacked time, structure, or clinical confidence.
  • Children needed repeated practice without anyone available to coach it.
  • The first 30 minutes of a 60-minute session disappeared into catch-up and guesswork.

02 · The Approach

I designed Wulo as an operating system for therapist-led care.

The question was not what can AI do? It was what should the OS run on behalf of the therapist, and what must the therapist still own?

01

Capture the signal.

If a practice event is not captured, it is invisible. I capture audio, transcripts, and pronunciation telemetry by default.

02

Compress for review.

Raw signal is not the deliverable. I run a pronunciation analyser that scores and summarises every session into a decision-ready record.

03

Therapist is the authority.

Every AI output is inspectable. Plans, scores, and notes are draft inputs; therapists approve, edit, or reject.

04

What I chose not to automate.

Diagnosis, goal setting, and discharge stay therapist-only. I never let the OS propose a clinical decision.

Approach · Tradeoffs

What I chose, what I rejected, and why.

Realtime stack

I chose Azure Voice Live.

Rejected: OpenAI Realtime API (no avatar, weaker en-GB child speech), LiveKit + Whisper (own infra, no integrated TTS, higher first-token latency).

Why: I get avatar, realtime ASR, and TTS in one session contract, with one vendor to operate.

Pronunciation pipeline

I chose Azure Speech PA with a custom PLS lexicon.

Rejected: Whisper + Montreal Forced Aligner (no phoneme confidence on child speech), bespoke acoustic model (cost and data unjustified at current scale).

Why: I get phoneme-level accuracy and reference IPA out of the box; PLS gives me the clinical /r/ target the default lexicon misses.

Storage

I chose PostgreSQL as source of truth.

Rejected: SQLite on Azure Files (SMB locking and corruption risk), Cosmos DB (overkill, weaker relational queries for session history).

Why: I get typed schemas, joins across child, session, and plan, and a managed HA path. I keep SQLite only as a local dev cache.

PhonemeScore

expected_ipa: text

Implementation · Realtime

The child gets a practice partner. The therapist gets evidence back.

  1. 1
    Capture

    AudioWorklet streams 24 kHz mono PCM in 20 ms frames to the backend over WebSocket. No WebRTC peer connection.

  2. 2
    Converse

    Voice Live returns assistant audio, avatar video, and per-turn transcription deltas. Server-side VAD with a 200 ms hangover lets the child barge in at any turn.

  3. 3
    Score

    Per utterance, I call Azure Speech pronunciation assessment to score accuracy, fluency, and completeness against target words, using a custom PLS lexicon.

  4. 4
    Persist

    I write transcript, phoneme scores, and AI assessment to Postgres as one typed SessionRecord; audio lands in Blob.

Latency budget
Target mouth-to-ear ≤ 850 ms p95: 20 ms capture frame, WebSocket hop < 30 ms, Voice Live first token < 350 ms, TTS playback start < 450 ms.
Implementation · Speech intelligence

Making speech AI work for clinical phonemes.

Off-the-shelf speech models were not enough for the clinical /r/ target. I built a phoneme-aware layer on top.

  • Target-sound model linking exercises to specific words, phonemes, and acceptable substitutions for the child's age.
  • Azure Speech pronunciation assessment for word-level accuracy, fluency, and completeness signals.
  • SSML pronunciation lexicon (PLS) so /r/ targets are produced, and evaluated, with the right reference phonemes.
  • I built an eval harness comparing baseline vs phoneme-aware behaviour on therapy-specific prompts.
Result on the /r/ target
+22%
Relative reduction in phoneme error rate versus Azure Speech PA default lexicon, on the clinical /r/ target set.
Baseline: Azure Speech PA, en-GB, default lexicon. Treatment: same model with custom PLS for /r/. Eval set: 160 utterances on /r/ targets, scored against an SLT-prepared reference (two raters, disagreements adjudicated).
Where my first approach failed

What broke and what I learned.

Hypothesis 1

Alias-only lexicon control.

Result: token synthesis collapsed into unrelated outputs such as variants of model and row.

Decision: rejected. Alias-only control was too weak to drive clinically sensitive drill speech.

Hypothesis 2

Initial phoneme pass.

Result: directionally better than alias-only, but recognition was still unstable across the target blends.

Decision: not production-grade. Held back from the staging rollout.

Hypothesis 3

Best-candidate phoneme sweep.

Result: row strong, rah acceptable, roo and ree weak.

Decision: shipped as baseline; follow-up scoped to SSML drill lines only.

Implementation · Review intelligence

Raw practice becomes a decision-ready record.

  • SessionRecord: session_id, child_id, started_at, duration_s, transcript[], phoneme_scores[], target_attempts[], audio_uri, ai_summary, therapist_notes.
  • PhonemeScore: word, expected_ipa, observed_ipa, accuracy, error_type.
  • AI session note: a draft summary of effort, clarity, retries, and a suggested focus for the next session. The therapist approves or edits before it is saved.
  • Therapist feedback sits on top of the record; I never overwrite raw evidence.
